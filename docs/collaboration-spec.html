01

The problem worth solving

Every other item on your feature list — tasks, reminders, a priorities dashboard, calendar sync — already exists in a dozen products. This one doesn't.

The formal record and the actual conversation live in different tools. Email is citable, structured, and slow; it carries authority. Chat is fast and expressive; it carries nothing. So the record gets written twice — once as the conversation that really decided things, once as the email that documents it — or, more often, the second write never happens.

That's the failure you described: strip out the bureaucracy and things stop being properly addressed. Not because the formality was load-bearing, but because the formality was the only thing producing a record. Remove it and you remove the paper trail with it.

If promoting a chat message to an official decision takes one click, the record stops being extra work. It becomes a byproduct of talking.

02

One timeline, two registers

A stage of work has a single thread. Inside it, messages come in two registers, and the difference is visible at a glance — in weight, in border, and in typeface.

Notes — the informal register

Ordinary chat. Fast, casual, disposable-feeling. Set in the interface sans, in a soft bubble with no border. Reactions, attachments, replies. This is the Telegram half.

Records — the formal register

A structured post that becomes part of the project's official history. Set in serif, bordered, badged with a type and a sequence number so it can be cited in conversation ("per R‑07"). Every record has:

  • A type — Decision, Approval, Request, Update, Sign‑off, or Blocker.
  • A status — open, accepted, declined, or superseded.
  • An author of record, and optionally a list of people who must acknowledge it.

Promote — the mechanic that matters

Any note becomes a record in one click. It carries the original wording, the original author, and the original timestamp, and it keeps a permanent link back to the note it came from. Nobody retypes anything into an email. The thing that was actually agreed, in the words it was actually agreed in, becomes the official record.

Why the typeface split does real work

Serif for records, sans for notes, in the same stream. You can tell at a glance which parts of a long thread are binding without reading a word of it. It's the cheapest possible signal for the most important distinction in the product.

03

Three contexts, strict walls

Everything — every project, every conversation — lives in a space, and every space has exactly one context. This is what lets work life and personal life share a product without contaminating each other.

Work

Clients, employers, boards

Both registers on. External members allowed. The audit view matters, and records drive stage status.

Personal

Family, friends, your own life

Informal by default; the formal register is available but hidden until asked for. A house move deserves stages too.

Private

Only you

Notes to self, drafts, solo projects. No members, ever. Never appears in anyone else's view.

"Connected but not affecting each other" is the hard requirement, so the isolation rules are explicit and testable rather than left to good intentions:

  1. A message never moves or copies between spaces. Forwarding creates a new message, with visible attribution, and only on deliberate action.
  2. Membership is per-space. Sharing a Work space with someone grants exactly zero visibility into any Personal space — including the existence of one.
  3. A person is one contact record but many conversations. Your manager in Work and your manager in Personal are two threads that never merge, and deleting the Work space leaves the Personal one untouched.
  4. Notification rules are set per context. Silence all Work after 19:00 without silencing your family.
  5. Search is scoped to the current context by default. Searching across contexts is opt-in and says so plainly.
  6. The unified dashboard is the one place all three meet — and every row there carries its context chip and can be filtered down to one world.
  7. Existence is hidden, not just contents. An assistant cannot see that a space they lack access to exists. "You have three spaces I can't open" is itself information.

The connection you're after is real but narrow: one inbox, one priorities view, one set of contacts underneath. Everything else stays walled.

Where the assistant boundary actually sits

The first draft of this spec implied the assistant wall runs along the Work/Personal line. Working through who we're actually building for, that's wrong.

A PA or EA absolutely does handle personal life. Booking family travel, remembering a spouse's birthday, managing household staff, the school run. That isn't an edge case, it's the job — and Kairos already ships a Relationship Calendar that tracks birthdays and anniversaries, which is personal data an assistant maintains. A blanket "assistants never see Personal" would break a feature you already have.

But medical, legal, financial, a conflict inside the family, a quiet job search — those need somewhere no assistant can reach, and the principal has to trust that absolutely.

The wall that matters isn't Work versus Personal. It's delegated versus private — and that line cuts across all three contexts.

  • Work — delegated by default. That's what the assistant was hired for.
  • Personal — delegated per space, opt-in. "Family logistics" yes; "Health" no. The EA books the holiday without seeing the oncologist thread.
  • Private — never delegated, by construction. Not a permission that was revoked and could be restored: there is no code path to add a member to a Private space at all.

That last point settles question 6. Private stays its own context precisely because the no-members property is guaranteed by the type rather than by a permission check that might one day be wrong. A principal shouldn't have to trust a boolean.

Role sets the default, not the ceiling

You're right that the role chosen at onboarding should inform access. The catch is that these roles don't form a hierarchy. A PA often has more personal reach than a Chief of Staff, who may be purely strategic. Encoding chief_of_staff > ea > pa as a permission ladder would misdescribe a lot of real working relationships.

So role picks the opening bundle, and the principal adjusts per space from there:

RoleDelegated by defaultCan delegate onward
PA All Work spaces, plus Personal spaces marked household or family No
EA All Work spaces, including those marked confidential No
Chief of Staff All Work spaces, including confidential Yes — can grant and revoke other assistants' access
All three Private — never, under any configuration Never for Private

Onward delegation is the one genuinely hierarchical capability, and it's the honest answer to "what makes a Chief of Staff different" — they coordinate the rest of the team, so they can hand out and withdraw access. Everything else is a starting position the principal tunes.

Whose world, not just which world

A VA with five clients needs their own life separate from every client, and each client separate from each other. Spaces belong to a principal; an assistant reaches them through membership and keeps their own three contexts alongside. So the context chip carries two facts — whose world and which context — and the priorities view can filter on either.

Projects and stages

A project belongs to a space and holds an ordered list of stages — the formal spine that the conversation hangs off.

Stage — Circulatenot started

Each stage has an owner, a due date, and a status: not_started, active, blocked, done. Each stage owns a thread.

The connection back to section 02: records can drive stage status. An accepted Sign‑off record closes its stage. An open Blocker record flips it to blocked and surfaces it on every member's dashboard. So the formal register isn't ceremony — it's the thing that actually moves the project, which is precisely what makes promoting a note worth doing.

05

Tasks

Tasks attach to a stage, a project, or nothing at all (a loose to-do in a space). Fields: title, assignee, due date, priority, and status — open, doing, blocked, done.

The important detail is where they come from. Any message becomes a task in one click, the same gesture as promoting to a record, keeping a link back to the message that spawned it. "Wale still owes me the FX numbers" becomes an assigned, dated task without leaving the thread — and when you open the task later, the conversation that explains it is one click away.

06

The priorities view

The one screen that spans all three contexts, and the answer to "everything in one place." It reuses what Kairos already computes rather than inventing a parallel world — today's bookings and the PA approval queue are already built and land here alongside the new material.

BandWhat lands there
Needs youRecords awaiting your acknowledgement or sign-off, Tier 3/4 booking approvals, direct @mentions, blocked stages you own
OverdueTasks and stages past their due date, oldest first
TodayConfirmed meetings, tasks due today, stages due today
Next 7 daysUpcoming meetings, tasks, stage deadlines, and relationship dates (birthdays, anniversaries — already built)

Every row carries its context chip. One filter collapses the view to Work alone for the working day, or Personal alone on a Sunday. Same data, different lens — never a different app.

07

Reminders and notifications

  • Per-context rules. Each context gets its own delivery mode — realtime, digest, or off — plus quiet hours. This is what makes one app for both lives survivable.
  • Registers notify differently. A note is ambient. A record addressed to you is not — it can chase, escalate, and appear under "Needs you" until acknowledged.
  • Reminders fire on task due dates, stage deadlines, and unacknowledged records, at intervals you set.
  • Delivery rides the existing email service, so everything is already visible in the Outbox tab during development. In-app is realtime; WhatsApp remains credential-gated and inert (see section 10).
08

Data model

New tables, in the same style as the existing schema — text UUIDs, ISO‑8601 timestamps, explicit foreign keys, nothing SQLite-specific, so the Postgres port stays a port.

TableKey columnsNotes
spaces id, owner_id, name, context, created_at context ∈ work | personal | private. The isolation boundary.
space_members id, space_id, user_id, role, status, invited_email Mirrors the existing memberships invite flow. role ∈ owner | member | guest.
projects id, space_id, name, description, status, created_at Always inside exactly one space.
project_stages id, project_id, name, position, status, owner_user_id, due_at Ordered by position. Status may be driven by records.
threads id, space_id, project_id, stage_id, kind, created_at kind ∈ stage | project | space | dm. Nullable parents let one table serve group chat and stage threads alike.
messages id, thread_id, author_id, body, register, record_type, record_status, record_seq, promoted_from_id, reply_to_id, created_at, edited_at The core table. register ∈ note | record. Record columns are null for notes. promoted_from_id is the audit link.
message_acks message_id, user_id, acked_at Who must acknowledge a record, and who has.
reactions message_id, user_id, emoji Informal register only — a decision isn't reacted to, it's acknowledged.
tasks id, space_id, project_id, stage_id, source_message_id, title, assignee_id, due_at, priority, status source_message_id preserves the link to the conversation that created it.
notification_prefs user_id, context, space_id, mode, quiet_from, quiet_to Set per context, overridable per space.

Reused unchanged: users, contacts, bookings, briefs, memberships, emails. A brief already attaches to a booking; under this model it can also attach to a stage, which is a small change with a lot of leverage.

10

What this deliberately isn't

Honest constraint · decided

Google Calendar and Workspace integration cannot be built here. It needs real OAuth client credentials, which this environment doesn't have. The architecture already exists in calendarSync.js and will keep reporting "not configured" rather than pretending to sync. Same for WhatsApp. When you have credentials, these become small jobs — but I won't ship something that looks connected and isn't.

Confirmed: leave it saying "not configured." No mock sync, no placeholder that implies a connection. Locked.

  • Not end-to-end encrypted. You said "privately," so this needs saying plainly: private here means access-controlled — the server can read message contents. Real E2E encryption is a different and much larger product decision.
  • Not realtime in the first build. Live delivery needs WebSockets. The first version polls, which feels fine for threads and bad for fast chat. Worth doing properly in a follow-up rather than badly now.
  • No mobile app. Responsive web only.
  • No AI in the thread yet. The existing assistant drafts messages and briefs; pointing it at thread history — summarizing a stage, drafting the record from a long argument — is an obvious follow-up, not part of this.

12

What I need you to decide

Three of six are settled. The remaining three still change what gets built.

Settled

1. Can a PA act inside their principal's Personal context? Decided

Yes, per space, opt-in — with role setting the opening defaults. Private stays structurally unreachable for every role. Detailed in section 03.

4. Who can promote a note to a record? Decided

Anyone with write access, because promotion is clerical, not authoritative. A record's weight comes from whose words it captures, not from who filed it — which is why every record shows both. In the section 02 mockup, Pam files R‑07 but it carries Elena's decision and Elena's name. Restricting promotion would only mean assistants couldn't do the filing that is precisely their job.

6. Is Private a context, or just a space with one member? Decided

Its own context. The guarantee has to be structural — no code path to add a member — rather than a permission that could be flipped. Reasoning in section 03.

Still open

2. Are records immutable once acknowledged?

Legal-grade means no edits after acknowledgement — only a superseding record. Stricter is more trustworthy and more annoying. Which side?

3. Can external clients participate without a Kairos account?

An email-bridge — they reply by email, it lands as a note — is powerful and roughly doubles the messaging build. In or out for now?

5. Do stages need templates?

If a Chief runs the same five-stage shape every time, a reusable project template saves real work. Worth building in 3B, or later?
