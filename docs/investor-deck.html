A day is not a list.
It is a chain.

Every calendar ever built stores appointments as independent rows. That single modelling decision is why an executive assistant still holds the whole day in their head — and why the software they were sold cannot help them at the one moment it matters.

Kairos · scheduling and custody for principals

01The observation

The 11:00 only works because the 10:00 ends at 10:45

Ask any assistant what happens when a meeting overruns by twenty minutes. They do not open a calendar. They run a chain in their head: does the next thing move, is there a gap that swallows it, is there anything today that physically cannot move, and who has to be told before they are standing outside a building waiting.

Every product in this category models the day as rows in a table. Move one row and one row moves. The 11:00 sits exactly where it was, on top of the meeting that just overran. The driver is still at the old time. Nothing anywhere says so. The principal finds out by being late.

The work an assistant is actually paid for is the part no calendar has ever modelled.

Kairos models it. A delay is computed against the whole rest of the day and shown before it is applied. Two rules carry most of the weight, and you can run both of them below on a real Lagos day.

03The wedge

One observation, and everything else follows from it

Once a day is a chain rather than a list, a set of features stop being features and start being consequences.

  • Travel time is part of the day, not a note on it. Ikoyi to Victoria Island is twelve minutes on a Sunday morning and eighty at six on a Thursday. Kairos asks the road, at the departure hour, with traffic — because the chain cannot be computed without it.
  • An anchor is a first-class idea. A flight departs when it departs. The most expensive thing a scheduling product can do is let somebody believe a plane will wait, so Kairos refuses to move one and says how late you would be instead.
  • Who to tell falls out of the maths. When three legs move, the driver is told once, not three times, and the external attendee is flagged for a human to message — never messaged automatically, because the wording of we are running late is a judgement call.
  • A trip is an object. A week in London means the day is drawn in London — start, end, gaps and cascade all reasoned in the zone the principal is actually standing in.

A competitor reading a feature list can build a delay button. What they cannot copy from a list is the model underneath it, because the list does not contain the model.

04The second market
Custody

The other thing an assistant holds is a passport number

The second half of the job has nothing to do with time. An airline wants a passport number and the name exactly as printed. A hotel wants a card. A visa form wants a BVN. A bank wants a different number from the one the telco wants. Today those live in a WhatsApp thread, a photo roll, and an assistant's memory.

Kairos holds them as answers, not documents — a field with a copy button, an expiry, and a sensitivity. Every field in the catalogue has already been classified: an assistant engaged for scheduling can see a nut allergy and cannot see a Bank Verification Number.

Encrypted with a key held outside the database

AES-256-GCM. A stolen copy of the data is not a stolen identity — the attacker needs the running server's environment too. Lose the key and the data is gone, by design: a recovery path is just a second key, usually a worse-kept one.

The second gate asks for the second factor

Revealing a passport number used to cost the account password — which defends against nothing, since the attacker who matters already has it. Where two-factor is enrolled, the vault asks for the phone in your pocket instead.

A calendar is switched. Custody of the number a bank asks for is not.

This is where the retention argument lives. Scheduling gets an office to try Kairos. Custody is why the office is still on it in year three — and it is the part a general-purpose competitor cannot bolt on late, because the sensitivity classification has to exist before the first field is stored.

05The moat

Four decisions that do not appear on a feature list

Each of these is structural — enforced by the shape of the data or the shape of the code, not by a flag somebody could flip without noticing what it touched.

404, not 403

Ask for a space you cannot see and Kairos answers no such thing, not not allowed. The existence of a private space is itself information the owner has not shared. Refusing tells you it is there.

Absent, not refused

A private space has no members by construction, and the access resolver short-circuits before it even consults the membership table — so a stray row from a future migration still cannot open a door that is not supposed to exist. Household staff live in their own table for the same reason: a cook added to the assistants table would have been handed the approval queue, the contacts and the vault at six query sites at once, none of which would have looked wrong in review.

Entitlement is never access control

Whether an account is on Plus is one question. Whether a delegate may read a BVN is a different one, decided somewhere else entirely. If those ever merge, a billing bug becomes a data breach and a lapsed card locks a principal out of their own passport at an airport. The plan layer fails open, deliberately.

Honest about what is not built

Every unbuilt capability appears in the product, by name, in the place the working control will occupy — and says so when pressed. It reads its status from the same environment the real feature reads, so the notice removes itself the moment the thing starts working. Nobody is ever shown a button that quietly does nothing.

With this audience that last one is an asset, not a liability. A team that will not overstate a build to a prospect will not overstate one to you.

06The market

Who pays, and for whom

The buyer is rarely the user. In almost every case the principal pays and the assistant uses it daily — which makes the assistant the champion and the principal the signature. Kairos is built for both sides of that, with separate home screens and a switcher for assistants running more than one desk.

The wedge into all four is the same: an assistant who is late once because a calendar did not know about traffic. That happens weekly, everywhere, and it is not a problem anybody has been sold a solution to.

Why Lagos first, and why that is an advantage

Nigeria has the hardest version of this problem and the least tolerance for a product that guesses. Traffic makes travel time the entire schedule rather than a rounding error. Identity is not one number but four — BVN, NIN, TIN, RC — asked for by name, by different institutions, in the same phone call. A driver meeting a principal at an airport with a name board is a targeting notice, which is why Kairos meets them with a phrase and a colour instead.

Build for that market and London is a simplification. Build for London and Lagos is a rewrite. The order matters, and it is the right way round.

09Next

What the money does

  1. Provider contracts. Maps, flight data, object storage, transcription and inbound mail. Seven of the named capabilities are one signed agreement away from live — the code is written and the notices remove themselves.
  2. Durable infrastructure. Managed Postgres with real backups before this holds a principal's calendar and identity documents, rather than the free tier that is fine for evaluation and nothing else.
  3. Design partners, paid attention rather than paid fees. Ten offices in Lagos and London, onboarded by us, for a quarter.
  4. The concierge network. The one part that is not software: vetted people who answer at 2am in the city the principal is actually in.

The engine is built. What is left to buy is reach, people, and the right to be trusted with the data.
