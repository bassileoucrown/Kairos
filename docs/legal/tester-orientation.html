Tester Orientation
Read once · Keep to hand · v1.0

You do not need training to use Kairos — every screen explains itself the first time you open it. This page covers the three things a screen cannot tell you: how to get your assistant in, what we are actually trying to learn, and why some of it will say “not yet”.

Start here — get both of you in

Kairos is only itself when the principal and the person who runs their diary are both on it. One of you alone tests half a product.

1
The principal signs up and finishes setup — handle, then a first meeting type.
2
Principal opens Account → Team and sets a pairing code: any phrase of six characters or more, like THURSDAY-LAGOS-91. Choose what it grants and how long it lives.
3
Read the code and your handle down the phone. Both are needed — the code alone opens nothing.
4
The assistant signs up as an assistant, lands on Workspace, and enters the handle and code there.

The code is off by default, live only for the window chosen, and spent after the joins allowed. If it stops working it has most likely expired — set another. Every wrong attempt gives the same message on purpose.

Finding your way

The rail is grouped, not one long list:

The dayToday, Itinerary, Trips, Movements, Calendar, Pad — what is happening and when to leave.
The deskWorkspace, Desk, Report, Correspondence — where an assistant works.
WorkSpaces and Tasks — talking and chasing.
The houseHousehold, Instructions, Connections.
AccountSettings, Team, Notices, Coming.

A principal lands on Today. An assistant lands on Workspace. That is not a mistake — you are meant to see different things.

The panel at the top of each screen

“What this does” opens the first time you visit a screen and folds itself afterwards. If you are ever unsure what you are looking at, press the + and it comes back. There is one on all 39 screens, and it is the manual — there is no other.

Why some of it says “not yet”

Kairos is finished software with parts that are switched off until we configure them — travel time, document storage, the AI drafting, and more. That is not a fault and you need not report it.

Account → Coming lists everything that is not available on your deployment, says which screen it will appear on and what it is waiting for, and removes each entry by itself the moment we switch it on. Trust that screen over this page — it is always current and this page is not.

You are seeing everything

Plan tiers are switched off for the pilot, so nothing is behind a paywall. Some of what you use freely now will sit on a paid tier later. We would rather you judged the whole product than a sample of it.

What we are actually asking

Run real work through it for two or three weeks. A diary nobody depends on teaches us nothing. Then tell us about:

  • The hand-over. Does the assistant have what they need without asking the principal for it?
  • Today. Opened once in the morning, does it tell the principal the shape of the day — including when to leave?
  • The desk. Do requests land in one place and get answered once, or do you still end up in WhatsApp?
  • Where you stopped. The screen you avoided, the thing you did the old way instead. That is the most useful thing you can tell us.

Small irritations count. “It took four taps” is a finding.

Telling us

Tell us sits at the foot of every screen. It sends your name and which screen you were on — never what you have written, and nothing from the vault. For anything longer, write to us directly.

We answer in Lagos hours and will say plainly whether a thing is being fixed, is queued behind something else, or is not going to be fixed.

Two things to know before you start

  • Keep your own copy of anything critical. The pilot runs on trial infrastructure that may not be backed up.
  • A private trip is genuinely private. Marked private, it is absent from the office entirely — not dimmed, absent — until you name who may see it. Worth testing on purpose.
