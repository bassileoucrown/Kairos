01The company

Exousia builds the software an executive assistant would build

Kairos is a scheduling and custody platform for principals — executives, high-net-worth individuals, family offices — and for the people who run their days: PAs, EAs and Chiefs of Staff. A Nigerian company, working on a problem that is not.

The name is deliberate. Chronos is clock time, the thing every calendar already stores. Kairos is the right moment — which is what an assistant is actually protecting when they move a meeting.

We are not building a prettier calendar. We are building the part the assistant currently holds in their head.

02The idea

A day is a chain, not a list

Every calendar stores appointments as independent rows. Move one, and one moves. But an 11:00 only works because the 10:00 ends at 10:45 and the car takes twenty minutes — so when the 10:00 overruns, an assistant runs a chain in their head that no software has ever run for them.

Here is a Thursday in Lagos where the board pre-read overruns by two hours. This is what Kairos shows, before anything is changed:

09:30–10:15 09:30–12:15 Board pre-readOverran by 120 min The cause
10:30–11:15 12:15–13:00 Call with counselAdaeze Okafor is expecting you Moves 105 min
12:15–13:30 13:30–14:45 Lunch, Eko HotelAllowing 30 min to get there Moves 75 min
14:15–16:45 15:00–17:30 Car to the airportEmeka is driving Moves 45 min
17:10 BA75 to LondonYou would reach it 20 min after it leaves Missed

Two rules produce all of that, and they are the whole engine:

  • A gap absorbs. If the next thing does not start for an hour, a twenty-minute overrun changes nothing and the ripple stops there. A system that shunts the whole afternoon regardless is worse than useless — it cries wolf, and people stop reading it.
  • An anchor does not move. A flight departs when it departs. Kairos will not quietly re-time one to make the arithmetic work. The most expensive thing this product could ever do is let somebody believe a plane will wait.

And the output is not three things moved. It is who has to be told — the driver once, however many of his legs changed, and the external attendee flagged for a human to message, never messaged automatically, because the wording of we are running late is a judgement call.

03The second half

The other thing an assistant holds is a passport number

Half the job has nothing to do with time. An airline wants a passport number and the name exactly as printed. A visa form wants a BVN. A bank wants a BVN, a telco wants a NIN, an invoice wants a TIN — three different numbers, asked for by name, often in the same phone call.

Kairos calls these essentials, and treats each one as an answer rather than a document: a field with a copy button, an expiry date, and a sensitivity. Everything is encrypted with a key that lives outside the database, and revealing a sensitive field asks for a second factor — not the account password, because the attacker who matters already has that.

The load-bearing idea is sensitivity per field. A delegate engaged for scheduling can see a nut allergy and a dietary note. They cannot see a BVN. That is decided once, in the catalogue, rather than at every screen that happens to show a field.

This is also the commercial answer to why would they not just switch back. A calendar is switched in an afternoon. Custody of the numbers an institution asks for by name is not.

04How we decide

Six principles, and they have teeth

These are not values on a wall. Each one has changed something in the product, and each one will be quoted at you in review.

  • 404, not 403.Ask for a space you cannot see and the answer is no such thing, not not allowed. Refusing tells you it is there, and the existence of a private space is itself information the owner did not share.
  • Absent, not refused.Where we can make a door structurally impossible rather than merely locked, we do. Private spaces have no members by construction. Household staff live in their own table so no existing query can include a driver by accident.
  • Never pretend.A feature that does not work says so, by name, in the place it will live. No greyed-out buttons that swallow clicks, no request box with nobody behind it. If we would have to guess, we say we do not know.
  • Entitlement is never access control.What plan an account is on and what a person may read are answered in different places, on purpose. If they ever merge, a billing bug becomes a data breach.
  • Fail open on money, fail closed on data.An unpaid month must never stop a principal reading their own passport number at an airport. A missing permission must always stop somebody reading someone else's.
  • The record is the product.Notes are conversation and can be edited. Records are commitments and cannot. That distinction is why an office would move off WhatsApp at all.
05The product
Today

What works right now

You can sign up today and do all of this. Nothing in this list is a mock-up.

The day

Today, itinerary, the delay cascade, travel time with live traffic, anchors, and one screen that answers what needs me.

Booking

Weekly availability, meeting types with four access tiers, a public booking page, reschedule and cancel, correct timezones throughout.

The team

PA, EA, Chief of Staff and delegate. An approval queue for the sensitive tiers. Pairing codes so an assistant can be brought on by phone, without email.

Essentials

The encrypted vault, sensitivity per field, expiry warnings, trip readiness, and two-factor step-up before anything sensitive is revealed.

Trips

A journey as an object: per-leg timezones, the flight chain, hotel, transfers, travellers, visa coverage, and the arrivals-hall signal instead of a name board.

The direct line

One room per principal with every active assistant in it. Typed or spoken. Notes, records, acknowledgement, and marking an instruction carried out.

Spaces and projects

Work, personal and private compartments. Stages driven by records — an open blocker moves the board, not a status dropdown.

Household and peers

Drivers, cooks and housekeepers with no reach into anything else. Connections between two assistants at two different offices.

The last two rows are the ones to understand properly. They are not backlog; they are refusals. We will not answer does a Nigerian passport need a visa for Kenya from a guess, because a wrong no strands somebody at a check-in desk with a boarding pass they cannot use. And a concierge request box with nobody behind it would be a lie told at the exact moment somebody was relying on us.

Say both of those out loud when you demo. They land better than anything on the built list.

08Where you fit

Your first week

  1. Use it as a principal. Sign up, set your hours, make a meeting type, book yourself from an incognito window. An hour.
  2. Use it as an assistant. Have someone invite you, or join with a pairing code. Notice how different the two home screens are — that is deliberate, and it is the thing competitors get wrong.
  3. Break a day. Build a Thursday with a flight at the end, then overrun the first thing by two hours. Watch the anchor refuse.
  4. Read /coming. Learn the seven unbuilt things by name before you ever describe the product to anyone outside.
  5. Then read the founder handbook. Everything above in full, with the reasoning. If you are writing code, read the developer handover as well.

How to talk about Kairos outside

  • Never say a thing works when it does not. This is the one rule with no exceptions, and it is also our best sales line.
  • Lead with the chain, not the feature list. The demo that lands is a day breaking, not a screenshot tour.
  • Do not promise dates for anything on the not-yet list. Three of those depend on a contract nobody has signed.
  • Credentials — connection strings, API keys, the encryption key — never travel through chat, email, or a document. They go from the provider straight into the deployment's environment. This includes talking to us.

We would rather ship less and be believed about all of it.
