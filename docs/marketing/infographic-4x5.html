Kairos.

The operating system for a principal’s day — and for the assistant who runs it.

Every other scheduling tool is built for the person doing the booking. Kairos is built for the person whose time is being protected, and the assistant who protects it.

Five systems, working as one

Booking with a door policy

Four tiers of access, so a stranger and a board member are never treated the same. Some meetings confirm themselves. Some arrive as a request your assistant approves — or declines.

A desk of their own for the assistant

Not a permission bolted onto someone else’s calendar. A real workspace: approvals, briefs, instructions, correspondence and minutes — across every principal they run.

The day, and getting through it

Today in order. Trips, movements, driver cards, check-calls, and travel time read from live traffic at the hour you actually leave — not a number somebody typed once.

Custody — the part nobody else has

Passports, visas, policies and the documents behind them. Encrypted before they leave the server, opened only with a second factor, and every single reveal written down.

Intelligence that refuses

It drafts, it catches you up, it reads the week ahead. It never sends on anyone’s behalf, and it cannot reach the vault at all.

Four things Kairos will not do

Show a scheduling assistant your passport number. They see the diary, not the drawer.
Tell the office where you are on a private trip. You choose who knows, one person at a time.
Open a sensitive detail without leaving a trace — and the trail is written for you to read.
Send anything as you. AI drafts, people send — and only a delegate you named may send at all.
